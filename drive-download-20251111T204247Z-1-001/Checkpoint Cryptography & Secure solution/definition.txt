Plaintext :
Plaintext is the original, readable message or data that you want to protect. It has not been encrypted, and anyone who sees it can understand it.


Ciphertext :
Ciphertext is the encrypted version of the plaintext. It appears unreadable or scrambled and is produced after applying an encryption algorithm.


Encryption :
Encryption is the process of converting plaintext into ciphertext using a cryptographic algorithm and a key, to prevent unauthorized access.


Decryption :
Decryption is the reverse process of encryption. It transforms the ciphertext back into its original plaintext using the correct key.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Symmetric cryptography uses the same key for both encryption and decryption, making it faster but requiring secure key distribution. Asymmetric cryptography uses a pair of keys—one public for encryption and one private for decryption—solving the key distribution problem but being slower.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Confidentiality: Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized parties.

Integrity: Verifying that data has not been altered during transmission or storage.

Authentication: Verifying the identity of users or systems involved in communication.
